# Authority and evidence pass

Make content more useful and trustworthy. Metadata and bylines cannot substitute for real expertise or proof.

## Scope

- Read the requested pages, voice guidance, and any term list or content strategy.
- Identify specific gaps: unclear concepts, unsupported claims, missing attribution, stale information, or inaccurate metadata.
- Propose the needed changes and obtain approval for new pages unless already authorized. Do not automatically create a glossary or rebuild every page template.

## Explanations

- Use inline definitions for small gaps and dedicated pages when the audience needs a fuller answer.
- Research technical claims using current primary sources appropriate to the topic.
- Follow `seo-writing-framework` for substantial copy and the site's existing publishing conventions.
- Include examples, limitations, related links, and comparison tables only when useful. A product section or FAQ is optional, not mandatory.

## Attribution and proof

- Use actual authors and verified credentials, experience, and sources. Do not fabricate firsthand use, reviews, quotations, or qualifications.
- Confirm the right person and permission before adding a bio, headshot, or personal details.
- Follow the site's byline, date, and design conventions; do not impose a component name, image size, URL pattern, or first-person voice.
- Keep publication dates truthful and update dates tied to real content changes.

## Metadata and discovery

- Apply project-specific titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, social images, navigation, and sitemap patterns where relevant.
- Choose structured data only when it accurately describes visible content and meets current provider eligibility rules; verify those rules before implementing.
- Reuse existing components and image generation. Do not require JSON-LD, a headshot, or a glossary on every page.
- Keep descriptions useful and accurate rather than forcing platform or differentiator keywords into them.

## Verify and hand off

- Run applicable content, link, schema, build, and rendered-page checks.
- Report changed/new URLs, evidence sources, validation, and unresolved approvals.
- Prepare a PR only when authorized; do not merge, deploy, or submit indexing requests without that authority.
- If Grain is connected, save research, approved copy, URL lists, and validation evidence in the shared task folder and pass its ID/storage rule downstream. Keep source changes in the project; local artifact copies are fine. Otherwise continue normally and preserve privacy.
