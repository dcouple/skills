# SEO foundations

Build reusable context for a new site, or refresh it when the audience or positioning changes.

## Understand the site

- Read the homepage and relevant product, pricing, documentation, and audience material.
- State what it offers, who it serves, the problem solved, and supported differentiators.
- Flag unclear messaging and assumptions; do not invent a competitive advantage.

## Find real alternatives

- Search where this audience actually discovers solutions. Examples: developer communities for infrastructure, trade publications for a specialist service, app stores for consumer software.
- Include manual workflows, agencies, and “do nothing” where they are real alternatives.
- Use buyer-language queries such as “[category] for [audience]” or “how to [task].” Follow evidence rather than a fixed query or competitor quota.
- Check current availability and fit. Lack of recent commits alone does not make a stable project obsolete.
- Ask the user to correct a materially uncertain competitor set; unexpected results may reflect search limitations as well as positioning.

## Map opportunities

- Sample relevant competitor pages, search results, and available performance data.
- Record their page types, intent, useful proof, and content gaps with dated sources.
- Recommend pages only when they serve the reader. Comparisons, explainers, or alternatives pages are options, not automatic first moves.
- Check relevant technical basics such as indexability, metadata, canonical URLs, and accurate structured data. A glossary or author page is not required for every site.

## Output

- Product/audience summary and open positioning questions.
- Competitor table: alternative, discovery source, reader fit, strengths, limitations.
- Search landscape: query, intent, current results, evidence gaps.
- Prioritized starting actions with rationale, proof needed, and expected effort.

Use the requested destination, otherwise `.seo/foundations.md`. Hand off to `seo-briefing`, `seo-content-strategy`, or `page-strategy` as appropriate; research does not authorize creating or publishing pages.
