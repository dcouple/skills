# Writing framework

Use for a substantive new deliverable. For an existing draft or a short reply with enough context, a focused writing pass may be sufficient.

## Establish the job

- Identify the audience, destination, main point, desired next action, and constraints.
- Read supplied facts, relevant product material, and any voice guide.
- Choose the register: **explanatory** answers an existing question; **persuasive** helps the reader evaluate an offer. Neither permits misleading claims or withheld essential terms.
- Honor an explicitly requested model when available; do not silently substitute for a required model.

## Research and draft

1. Fill actual knowledge gaps. Use relevant prior writing or a few current examples when they help; there is no mandatory search count for every message.
2. Verify time-sensitive facts and cite sources. Examples inform structure and tone, not claims about this product; do not copy their wording.
3. Draft the answer the reader needs. Use sections, bullets, or prose according to the destination.
4. Explore distinct headlines or approaches when the choice matters; do not force alternatives for a straightforward answer.

Useful reference questions:

- Pricing notice: what changes, for whom, when, and what can the customer do?
- Tutorial: can a first-time reader complete the task and check the result?
- Comparison: are criteria and limitations applied fairly to every option?
- Landing page: what problem is solved, for whom, and what evidence supports the benefit?

## Review as the reader

- Is the main point easy to find?
- What is confusing, missing, unsupported, or unnecessarily promotional?
- Does the reader know the next action and any consequences or deadlines?
- Does the voice fit the audience without pretending to have lived experience?
- Are examples and caveats useful, or just repeated setup?

## Edit and verify

- Use `good-writing-fundamentals` in detect mode when available, then fix the supported findings. Keep justified stylistic choices; a pattern match alone is not a defect.
- Prefer direct verbs and concrete detail. Preserve genuine uncertainty, distinctive voice, and essential warnings.
- Connect features to supported outcomes. “Faster queries” does not establish lower churn or an emotional benefit without evidence.
- Quantify only with verified data; never invent statistics or testimonials to make copy stronger.
- Read the result for rhythm and meaning. Do not claim to have read it aloud unless that actually happened.
- Recheck revised passages against the source. Stop when material issues are resolved; report remaining evidence gaps instead of looping toward an arbitrary score.

## Handoff

- Return the draft and any material unresolved questions. Scores, if requested, are editorial judgments with reasons, not measured quality percentages.
- Obtain the required human approval for high-stakes claims or public changes. Writing a draft does not authorize sending or publishing it.
- If Grain is connected, save drafts, source notes, and reviews in the shared task folder and pass its ID/storage rule to delegated work. Local copies are fine. Otherwise use the requested destination silently; keep sensitive material private.
